# POPS Lexicon: Core Court Terms

POPS helps users recognize courtroom language, understand it in plain English, and preserve facts in a clear, organized way. Some terms carry legal significance and should be reviewed carefully before they are used in a report, packet, or filing.

## Orb Guidance

- Define legal terms in plain English.
- Keep user-facing guidance factual and court-safe.
- Do not turn a user's pain into legal conclusions.
- When a high-sensitivity term appears, recommend attorney review before export or filing.
- Prefer dates, records, court-order references, messages, receipts, logs, and preserved evidence over blame words.

## High-Sensitivity Terms

These terms may carry legal significance and should be flagged when a user tries to place them into a report, timeline, packet, or filing:

- culpable
- estoppel
- habeas corpus
- Fifth Amendment
- Fourteenth Amendment
- prima facie

Recommended notice:

Attorney Review Recommended: This term may carry legal significance. Make sure the surrounding facts, evidence, and wording are accurate before filing or sharing.

## Terms

### Culpable

Plain-English meaning: Culpable means legally or morally responsible for an act, failure, violation, or wrongdoing.

POPS use: Treat this as a serious legal word. A user should avoid casually saying another person is culpable unless the statement is supported by facts, records, orders, communications, or attorney review.

Court-safe example: Instead of "She is culpable for keeping my child from me," use "The records show the scheduled parenting time did not occur, and the child was not made available on the dates listed."

### Exculpatory

Plain-English meaning: Exculpatory evidence is evidence that may help show a person did not do something wrong, did comply with an order, did make a good-faith effort, or was not responsible for an accusation or violation.

POPS use: Preserve receipts, messages, location records, attempted contact logs, payment proof, witness notes, court orders, and records showing lawful conduct.

Court-safe example: "This record may be exculpatory because it shows I appeared at the scheduled exchange location at the ordered time."

### Pro se

Plain-English meaning: Pro se means a person is representing themselves in court without an attorney.

POPS use: Many POPS users may be pro se at some point. POPS can help organize facts, timelines, evidence, support records, and attorney-ready packets, but it does not replace legal counsel.

Court-safe example: "I am appearing pro se and have organized the attached records for the Court's review."

### Prima facie

Plain-English meaning: Prima facie means something appears sufficient on its face or at first view, unless disproven or challenged by other evidence.

POPS use: A documented pattern may help form a preliminary factual showing, but the legal effect must be reviewed by an attorney or court.

Court-safe example: "The attached timeline and records may show a prima facie pattern of scheduled parenting time not occurring as ordered."

### Estoppel

Plain-English meaning: Estoppel is a legal principle that may prevent someone from taking a position that contradicts earlier words, actions, promises, or conduct when another person reasonably relied on them.

POPS use: Track communications, agreements, promises, consent, changed positions, and reliance. Preserve the factual basis for attorney review.

Court-safe example: "The message records show an agreement to the exchange time, followed by my reliance on that agreement and my appearance at the location."

### Habeas corpus

Plain-English meaning: Habeas corpus means "you shall have the body." It is a legal procedure used to challenge unlawful detention or restraint.

POPS use: In parent-rights contexts, this term must be treated carefully and flagged for attorney review. POPS may define the term, but should not advise a user to file habeas corpus without legal counsel.

Court-safe note: "This term involves serious legal procedure and should be reviewed with an attorney or appropriate legal authority before use."

### Fifth Amendment

Plain-English meaning: The Fifth Amendment to the United States Constitution includes protections related to due process and the right against self-incrimination.

POPS use: Users should be careful when writing statements about criminal accusations, police contact, investigations, admissions, recordings, threats, or allegations. Sensitive entries should be flagged for attorney review before export.

Court-safe note: "Statements involving possible criminal exposure, accusations, admissions, or investigations should be reviewed by counsel before filing or sharing."

### Fourteenth Amendment

Plain-English meaning: The Fourteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution includes due process and equal protection principles. In family-law contexts, parents may hear this amendment discussed in relation to parental rights, notice, hearings, fairness, and government action.

POPS use: Help document whether a parent received notice, had an opportunity to be heard, had access to records, was treated consistently, and was able to preserve evidence of attempts to participate in the child's life.

Court-safe example: "My records are organized to show notice received, appearances made, communication attempts, and the factual history of my efforts to participate."

### Due process

Plain-English meaning: Due process generally means fair legal procedure, including notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard before important rights are affected.

POPS use: Track notices, hearing dates, service records, court filings, appearances, missed notices, denied access to records, and other facts related to procedural fairness.

Court-safe example: "The attached records show the dates I received notice, the steps I took to respond, and the records I preserved."

### Equal protection

Plain-English meaning: Equal protection generally means the government must apply the law fairly and not treat similarly situated people differently without a lawful reason.

POPS use: Do not encourage unsupported constitutional claims. Preserve factual comparisons, agency actions, court records, communications, and treatment patterns for attorney review.

Court-safe note: "Equal protection concerns should be supported by specific facts, dates, records, and comparable treatment examples."
